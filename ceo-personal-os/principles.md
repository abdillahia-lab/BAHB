# Operating Principles

These are the principles that govern how this system works—and ideally, how you work.

Read them. Edit them. Make them yours.

---

## On Time

**Time is the only non-renewable resource.** Money can be made again. Reputation can be rebuilt. Energy can be restored. Time only moves in one direction.

**Protect your mornings.** The first three hours of your day have disproportionate impact. Guard them like the scarce resource they are.

**Calendar audits reveal priorities.** What you say matters is irrelevant. Your calendar shows what actually matters. Review it honestly.

---

## On Decisions

**Most decisions are reversible.** For reversible decisions, move fast. For irreversible ones, slow down and think clearly.

**Decision fatigue is real.** Reduce the number of decisions you make, not the quality. Routines, defaults, and delegation are tools for this.

**The cost of indecision compounds.** A good decision now usually beats a perfect decision later. Especially for reversible choices.

---

## On Energy

**Energy management trumps time management.** An hour of high-energy focus produces more than four hours of depleted effort.

**Know your energy patterns.** When are you sharpest? When do you crash? Design your day around reality, not aspiration.

**Energy is contagious.** The people you spend time with affect your energy. Choose carefully.

---

## On Focus

**What you don't do defines you as much as what you do.** Every yes is a no to something else. Choose your yeses deliberately.

**Most work is noise.** In any given week, a small number of actions create most of the value. Identify them. Protect them.

**Deep work requires protection.** Shallow work expands to fill available time. Block time for thinking, creating, and strategic work.

---

## On Goals

**Goals are guesses about what will make you happy.** Hold them lightly. Adjust when you learn more about yourself.

**Process beats outcome.** You control your actions, not results. Design good processes and trust them.

**The goal behind the goal matters more.** Always ask: "Why do I want this?" The underlying motivation often reveals a simpler path.

---

## On Relationships

**Relationships are the ultimate ROI.** Nothing compounds like trust built over decades.

**Your network is a reflection of you.** Not in a status way—in a values way. You become like the people you spend time with.

**Key relationships require key attention.** Spouse, children, close friends, key team members—these deserve deliberate, protected time.

---

## On Health

**Your body is the vehicle for everything else.** Neglecting it is borrowing from the future at a high interest rate.

**Sleep is not negotiable.** Chronic sleep deprivation impairs judgment, creativity, and emotional regulation. The things CEOs need most.

**Movement is maintenance.** You don't exercise to optimize. You exercise to maintain baseline function.

---

## On Money

**Money is a tool, not a goal.** Enough money buys freedom. More than enough often buys complexity.

**Know your number.** What does financial freedom actually require? Be specific. Then you can make rational tradeoffs.

**Lifestyle creep is silent.** Your expectations adjust faster than your awareness. Audit regularly.

---

## On Meaning

**Legacy is not achievement.** What you build matters less than who you become and how you treat people.

**Contribution outlasts accumulation.** The things you give—mentorship, opportunity, kindness—often matter more than the things you keep.

**Meaning is found, not manufactured.** It emerges from engagement with the world. You can't think your way to purpose.

---

## On Systems

**Systems beat willpower.** Design your environment and routines so the default action is the right action.

**Review what you track.** Tracking without reviewing is just data hoarding. If you measure it, look at it.

**Simple systems survive.** Complex systems fail when life gets hard. Keep it minimal. Keep it maintainable.

---

## On This System

**Consistency beats intensity.** A 5-minute daily check-in, done consistently, beats a 2-hour annual review done once.

**Reflection is not navel-gazing.** It's quality control for your life. Without reflection, you repeat mistakes instead of learning from them.

**The system serves you.** If something isn't working, change it. If something is missing, add it. This is a tool, not a religion.
